Plate I · The Gold Wyvern (Dracunculus fulgur)

Unlike true dragons, wyverns are not innately magical. They have no mana pathways to bend physics, or conjure the elements from thin air. Yet high in the alpine ridges of the Wildlands, Dracunculus fulgur, the Gold Wyvern, hunts with bolts of lightning. It casts no spells. It evolved a biological power plant.

Along its ribcage run two massive organs: the thoracic electroplaques, which hunters call Dynamo Sacs. They are stacked columns of muscle cells, like those of an electric eel, scaled up to apex proportions. Every downstroke of its twenty-eight-foot wings squeezes the sacs, and drives charged ions across thousands of cell walls. Flight itself charges the battery.

To use that charge, the Gold Wyvern swallows copper ore, quartz grit, and Sylvan iron as gizzard stones. Its stomach acid dissolves them, and the metals harden its hollow, copper-enamel fangs, and translucent amber scales. When its jaws snap shut, piezoelectric crystals in the palate strike a spark, ionising the air before its snout, and the stored charge leaps out in a blinding arc.

To keep from frying its own nerves, its heart and brain sit in triple sheaths of insulating fat. And beneath its scales live colonies of metallic-blue Static-Ticks: mites that feed on stray surface charge, and secrete a waterproof wax, so fog and storm rain never short-circuit the wyvern in flight.

Because this lightning is biology, not magic, anti-magic wards and mage-breaking strikes do nothing against it. Guild survival doctrine is simple. Drop all steel. Press yourself flat against dry stone. Aim a wooden-shafted bolt at the uninsulated underbelly. Pierce a Dynamo Sac, and the beast's own charge stops its heart.
